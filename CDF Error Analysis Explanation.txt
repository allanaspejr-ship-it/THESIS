CDF ERROR ANALYSIS EXPLANATION
================================================================================

What the graph shows
- The graph is a cumulative distribution function (CDF) of absolute forecast errors during the testing set.
- The x-axis is absolute error in MW, computed as |actual generation - predicted generation|.
- The y-axis is cumulative probability, or the percentage of testing predictions whose absolute error is less than or equal to the x-axis value.
- A curve that rises faster and stays farther to the left indicates better forecasting accuracy because more predictions have smaller errors.

How to interpret the result
- RBFNN has the leftmost/fastest-rising curve, so most of its testing errors are smaller than the benchmark errors.
- Random Forest and XGBoost rise more slowly, meaning a larger fraction of their testing predictions require more MW error before reaching the same cumulative probability.
- The percentile values summarize this: P50 is the median absolute error, P90 means 90 percent of errors are at or below that value, and P95 means 95 percent of errors are at or below that value.

Data used
- This CDF uses the testing-set day-ahead prediction files, not the July 01, 2025 operational forecast output.
- Actual generation values come from the historical testing set actual_generation column.
- Predicted values come from each model predicted_generation column.
- Files used:
  Thesis Forecasting\metadata\day_ahead_backtest\rbfnn_testing_day_ahead_predictions.xlsx
  Thesis Forecasting\metadata\day_ahead_backtest\random_forest_testing_day_ahead_predictions.xlsx
  Thesis Forecasting\metadata\day_ahead_backtest\xgboost_testing_day_ahead_predictions.xlsx

Does it refer to total cascade or each AGUS plant?
- This figure is NOT a total-cascade CDF.
- It pools all plant-level testing predictions from AGUS 1, AGUS 2, AGUS 4, AGUS 5, AGUS 6, and AGUS 7 into one model-level error distribution.
- Therefore, each curve summarizes plant-level testing forecast errors across all AGUS plants for one model.
- A separate total-cascade CDF would require first summing actual and predicted generation across all plants by timestamp, then computing cascade-level absolute error.

Thesis-ready interpretation
The CDF of absolute testing errors shows that the RBFNN model produced smaller errors for a larger proportion of testing observations than Random Forest and XGBoost. Since the RBFNN curve rises more steeply near the origin and reaches high cumulative probabilities at lower MW error values, it indicates better reliability under the day-ahead testing setup. The analysis is based on historical testing-set actual generation and model predictions for each AGUS plant, pooled across the six plants.
